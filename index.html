    Fusion in one minute

    Fusion only works if you can hold something hotter than the Sun's core in mid-air. Magnets do that.

    1. Fusion joins atoms to release energy

      A fusion power plant heats hydrogen fuel into a plasma of about 150 million °C, hot enough for atoms to fuse and release energy. That heat makes steam and then electricity, as in a conventional power plant.

    2. Nothing can touch the plasma, so magnets hold it

      No material survives contact with plasma that hot. Most designs, called tokamaks, hold it inside a doughnut-shaped vacuum vessel using strong magnetic fields.

    3. The magnets are superconductors

      A superconductor carries electric current with no resistance once it is cooled far enough. ITER's magnets, the largest ever built, weigh 10,000 tonnes and run at about −269 °C.

    4. There are two families of fusion magnet

      ITER-style reactors use low-temperature superconductors: wire made of niobium and tin (Nb₃Sn). Newer compact designs use high-temperature superconducting tape (REBCO), which makes stronger fields. Commonwealth Fusion Systems reached 20 tesla with it in 2021, which lets a reactor be much smaller.
