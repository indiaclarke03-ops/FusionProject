The Magnet Problem

Fusion promises clean power. Follow one magnet back to the mine.

Governments and fusion companies plan these materials as a security problem: can we get it? This site asks the question that conversation leaves out: who paid for it? A supply chain built on human rights abuse is not acceptable, however secure it is.

      Policy brief

      Traceability before scale

      Problem

      Fusion materials policy is framed around supply security. Human rights do not appear in the main industry and government supply chain discussions . Fusion's demand for these materials is tiny (a few tonnes of yttrium), so fusion buyers have little leverage alone.

      Principle

      People first. A material sourced through documented abuse fails the test regardless of how secure it makes supply.

      Proposals

      1. Conditions on public fusion money

        Public grants, milestone payments and offtake agreements for fusion require suppliers to trace magnet and plasma-facing materials back to at least the separation or smelting stage, and to disclose origin.

      2. A cross-sector due diligence pool

        Fusion developers buy rare earths, tin and niobium through verified channels shared with larger buyers that face the same chokepoints, to gain leverage none has alone.

      3. Tech proposal: Fusion Supplier Check

        A tool where a buyer enters a supplier and sees its upstream exposure, using Sayari corporate and trade data, scored on the Exposure Pathway framework. Iteration 1 describes it; iteration 2 builds it.
